# shorts-factory — project brief

## What this is
A factory that produces faceless YouTube Shorts in Spanish, engineered to
maximize views and retention. Every technical choice serves that goal. The
reference format is Zack D Films' stylized 3D "how it works" explainers —
we copy the mechanics, never scripts or footage: all scripts are original.

- **Topic-level outcomes are never escalated to the owner (owner statement, 2026-11-08):** the owner is not attached to any topic or idea. Re-staging a topic, returning it and replacing it from the idea bank are lens and system decisions under standing doctrine. ## Format policy (lens decision 2026-11-04, from production 012; full policy, reasons and reversal evidence: docs/current/specs/format-policy.md)
One fixed signature, five structures, three staging modes. The signature is voice, identity, typography, subtitles, hook formula, one hero or mechanism, dynamics inside the shot, one reframe and the closing slot. There is no fixed scene count (the former 6-7 scene template is retired): the mechanism sets it, 4-10 as a sanity range, and scenes are never padded or merged to hit a number. Each production declares `structure_variant`: `chain`, `journey`, `scale`, `fork` or, from production 019, `story` (Historia: ONE complete real story with a mechanism heart, sourced fact by fact, never serialized). From 019 the design lens also records a staging mode, `stage`, `world` or `cutaway`; in any five consecutive productions no mode appears more than three times, and the mode changes no gate. Never three in a row with the same variant or the same scene count. Closing: reframe, then one sentence containing the fixed word "envíaselo" with its word-locked tag; the sentence around it varies, ends on the person, and the archetype names a behaviour, not a belief; `fork` repeats the opening question. Where older text in this file describes a fixed structure or a fixed closing sentence, this wins. Applies to future productions; published videos are untouched. Earliest review 2026-12-01, against the bars set in the policy. **Precedence (widening of 2026-11-10):** from production 019, where older live text and the widened format policy disagree, the widened policy governs; productions 015-018 stay exactly as armed.

## Format doctrine (channel research, Oct 2026; evidence in docs/current/research/reference-channels.md)
- Vertical 9:16. Duration, by topic family (measured pace of the channel
  voice: ~2.9 words/s on the pilot): standard target 45–60 s of narration
  (~130–170 words); body/medical may run 40–50 s (~115–145 words);
  impossible numbers/scale 50–60 s (~145–170 words). This supersedes the
  earlier 30–35 s target: the reference accounts run 36–95 s. Historia
  (`story`, from production 019) runs 55–75 s (~160–215 words).
- Title: short question or "How X works", second person when natural,
  exactly ONE trailing emoji as an emotion label. No hashtags. Forward SEO
  rules (marketing lens, 2026-11-08; "Search and packaging" below) refine
  the title and description of future productions.
- Hook formula: the first sentence is a question (¿…?) about a TANGIBLE
  EVERYDAY OBJECT the viewer has already seen, plus an explicit FALSE
  BELIEF to bust. Approved openings by topic family: objects and body
  "¿Por qué…"; numbers "¿Por qué…", "¿Cuántos…", "¿Cuántas…", "¿Sabías
  que…"; whatif "¿Qué pasaría si…", "¿Por qué…". "¿Cómo…?" openers
  flopped in the references. Hook in the first 2 seconds. Reveal order: build
  the object up step by step — never show the finished phenomenon first.
- Structure: one hero object or one mechanism chain; each scene
  visualises ONE step of the mechanism (a new fact AND a new visual per
  step, roughly every 5–9 s). Numbers are carried by animated Editor
  overlays, not only spoken.
- Dynamics live INSIDE the shot, not in fast cutting: transformation arcs
  (start state → end state), camera movement (travels, orbits, tracks),
  multiplying/exploding objects, and one x-ray/cross-section reveal per
  "how it works" video. A slow push-in on an otherwise static action is a
  defect. Flat 2D is an anti-pattern (the reference flops were flat 2D).
- Closing: a spoken REFRAME that renames the object or fact ("they are
  not hairs, they are the print of each air bubble"), then follow, then
  "send this to that specific friend who…" naming a concrete person
  archetype (production field share_archetype). A twist in the last 3 s
  loops back to the hook; dilemma videos repeat the opening question.
- Topic families (the channel's portfolio, rotated under one format,
  voice and brand; every script is tagged with its family): (a) everyday
  objects with a hidden mechanism, (b) body/medical how-it-works,
  (c) impossible numbers/scale, (d) what-if/dilemmas. Avoid generic
  textbook science with no everyday anchor (tornado, GPS flopped) and
  off-promise topics (news, sports, labour law). Prefer topics the
  viewer can verify or try on the spot.
- Audience: pan-Hispanic. Neutral Spanish, universal topics and objects
  that exist identically in every Spanish-speaking country; no
  country-specific systems and no local slang in scripts or titles.
- Visual style: the REFERENCE LOOK (see "Reference doctrine" below):
  dark low-key stage, stylized 3D, NOT photorealistic, no real skin
  texture, no blood, no gore. Realistic graphic content risks YouTube
  age-restriction (kills reach and monetization) and disgust-skips; in
  this format the gross-out is conceptual, not visual. Our pilot v1
  rendered photorealistic — that is the anti-pattern to avoid. The
  earlier bright "Pixar-style" lavender stage is RETIRED.
- The style string of every model-generated production MUST contain
  "no text on screen". Labels, counters and numbers are Editor overlays,
  never model-generated text (validated by scenes_schema.py).
- Scene prompts must spell out the ACTION (what moves, enters, exits,
  where): video models render nouns well and actions badly. Add an
  explicit "never ..." clause for the plausible wrong reading. Every
  scene prompt is prefixed by the production's identical style string.
- Narration: Spanish, channel voice es-CL-LorenzoNeural (edge-tts) until
  the owner decides otherwise. Voice-led audio: fast continuous
  narration, no prominent music bed; clip audio only when it is genuine
  mechanism foley (see the audio gate).
- Typography (owner 2026-10-27): Montserrat ExtraBold (Google Fonts, SIL
  OFL; channels/mecanalia/assets/fonts/Montserrat-VF.ttf, logged in
  channels/mecanalia/assets/fonts/LICENSES.md) for subtitles, counters, labels and
  chips; non-spoken subtitle words use SemiBold of the same family.
- Subtitles: burned in, upper band centred at SUBTITLE_Y_CENTER = 0.24 of
  the frame height (a channel constant that moves to the channel config),
  short phrases (max 4 words) with the WHOLE current phrase visible; the
  currently spoken word sits in a dark rounded box with light text, the
  other words light grey with a thick black outline. Size and position
  are tuned constants in editor.py.
- Captions/descriptions are mini-hooks (hook + twist), not bare text or
  links; every script ships with a caption draft.
- Engine architecture: three visual engines — t2v (fal text-to-video),
  keyframes (fal stills animated by the Editor), code3d (3D drawn with
  code, rendered locally). The factory assigns engines AUTOMATICALLY; the
  owner is never asked to choose one. Selection is at VIDEO level: each
  production declares ONE primary_engine. The only per-scene exception is
  engine_override "keyframes" inside a t2v production (both are
  diffusion-family and share a photographic look). code3d is NEVER mixed
  with diffusion footage: a code3d production is code3d in every scene.
  scenes_schema.py rejects violations before any spend. The binding
  selection rules are in "Engine selection" below.

## Search and packaging (lens decision 2026-11-08, forward-only; full rules and reasons: docs/architecture.md "Search and packaging policy")
Applies to productions packaged after 2026-11-08; published videos and their packages are never edited. **Title:** the query a viewer types, in the approved question form, object or mechanism word in the first words, plain neutral Spanish, short enough to show whole, one trailing emoji, no hashtags. **Description:** line 1 restates the question and gives the answer in one clause; line 2 the twist; then the share line; hashtags follow the standing hashtag policy unchanged. **Playlist (owner correction 2026-11-08, supersedes the four family playlists):** the channel's public surface is ONE channel with ONE promise, and it has exactly ONE public playlist, "Empieza aquí": the channel's published videos, strongest performers first, as measured by the channel's analytics. The four topic families are an internal production taxonomy (topic selection and balance) and are never public channel structure. The playlist is created on the platform by the owner, never from this repo.

## Reference doctrine (binding, owner 2026-10-26)
Adopted from the reference study of three example channels
(docs/current/specs/reference-style-spec.md = the LOOK, docs/current/specs/reference-format-spec.md =
the STORYTELLING GRAMMAR, with frame evidence for every rule; both specs
are binding, this section is their summary). Where this section and an
older paragraph disagree, this section wins.

Identity change (the lavender stage is RETIRED; channels/mecanalia/brand.yaml
visual_identity carries the values):
- DARK stage: near-black warm void ~#120F10 (cross-section backdrop
  navy #16202C). Never a light or pastel stage.
- ONE accent: gold/amber (#F5C04A / #ECA432) = the ACTIVE QUANTITY
  (energy, charge, tension, growth, the thing being measured).
  Everything passive is GREY (#2D2A2B / #51555A / #DAD8D7). Teal
  (#67CDBE) = annotation graphics only (labels, chips, frames). Red
  (#BE4A4A) = error, failure, overflow. Yellow-green (#CBD764) =
  bacteria/biology. A part stays grey until the narration activates it.
- Productions v1-v6 (lavender, lace-world, toon, glossy boot) are
  FROZEN RECORDS of the retired identity: never modified, never reused
  as a style reference.

Look rules (style spec; evidence cited there):
1. Low-key frames; one soft key light plus a warm rim halo behind the
   hero; emissive accents are the second light. The stage is dark but the SUBJECT
   is LUMINOUS (owner 2026-10-27: "too dark overall, edges included"):
   lifted shadows, no heavy vignette (unify `lift` curve, vignette_angle 0),
   stage never a pure void.
2. Hero models simplified and chunky; detail only on the feature the
   fact is about; scale exaggerated so the mechanism is legible.
3. Energy and charge are emissive glowing tubes with bloom.
4. Cross-sections are flat clean cuts with a layer stack and tiny parts;
   cutaways get a glass plate and a cyan/teal frame.
5. The camera never rests: push-through (into a surface), pull-back
   (multiplication/context), or orbit/tilt. Scene changes are
   push-throughs or blur wipes, not bare cuts.
6. Caption band: the top ~25% of the frame is reserved; the hero is
   centred in the lower 70% with large dark negative space.
7. Text system: subtitles of 1-4 words with the spoken word bold white,
   the others grey (~55%), key words in a selection box; tiny ALL-CAPS
   labels with hairline leader lines (1-2 words); monospace counters;
   teal chips (figures/units only, never sources). BUILT in factory/editor.py +
   factory/overlays.py (Editor v2, 2026-10-26; see "Data overlays").
8. ONE hero rig per video, revisited and re-lit with new data, +1 new
   element per fact. End with a dimmed end card (~25% brightness,
   animated logo, @handle, ~4 s).

Storytelling rules (format spec; each narration line becomes a visual
EVENT, never a static illustration):
1. Every sentence changes something on screen; no 3 s window without an
   object, number or camera change.
2. The hook stages the false belief as a CONTRAST in the first 2-3 s:
   two actors on one stage, one of them fails.
3. "Inside / under / made of" = push through the surface into a cutaway
   or exploded layers, active part in the accent colour.
4. "Each / all / many" = multiplication with a pull-back.
5. EVERY NUMBER IS A SCREEN OBJECT (counter, chip, bar, year) that
   appears on the spoken word. A number is NEVER invented: an unsourced
   figure is flagged to the owner in the report (in a Historia production this covers every load-bearing story fact: format-policy.md). SOURCES ARE NOT RENDERED
   ON SCREEN (owner rule 2026-10-27, binding for every future production):
   no citation lines, no "ficha técnica / fuente" chips, no brand-name
   credit cards. Numbers stay as counters/chips/bars and are understood
   as approximate; the source lives in the beat sheet and report, and the
   spoken narration may name it. Counters, labels and dynamic comments
   stay. overlays.validate_scene_overlays rejects type "citation".
6. "But / what if not" = a counter-actor on the same stage with the same
   readout.
7. "Imagine..." = a named-person micro-scene; set it up early, return to
   it as the payoff.
8. The closing reframe is ONE single-image visual; then the CTA is acted
   out in the visual world (follow, share sheet, sent) naming the
   share_archetype, then the end card.
9. Event timing is word-locked (+-0.3 s) to the highlighted subtitle
   word.
10. Transitions carry meaning (blur wipe / push-through).
A production brief is a BEAT SHEET approved by all involved lenses (see "Governance") BEFORE any
generation: every narration line mapped to an event type with its word
lock, the hero-rig element ledger, every number with its source (or
flagged), and the CTA/end card.

## Pipeline
Deterministic code wherever money or precision is involved; LLMs only
where judgment is involved.
- factory/producer.py — scenes.json → fal.ai clips. Estimate before
  spending; >US$1 requires the owner's explicit OK; US$3 hard cap per
  run; max 2 attempts per scene; scenes with a valid clip are skipped
  (runs are resumable, never pay twice); every generation is logged to
  the production's costs.json at the normal (non-promo) rate; one fixed
  seed per production; prompt_expansion always "disabled". scenes.json
  is validated by factory/scenes_schema.py BEFORE any estimate or network
  call: an incomplete or contradictory file aborts with zero spend. The
  final prompt is: style string + scene prompt + transformation + camera
  + audio_style (t2v/keyframes routes). The producer implements t2v only
  so far; other routes abort before spend until they are built.
- Scene schema (scenes.json), all REQUIRED unless noted. Production
  level: primary_engine (t2v | keyframes | code3d), topic_family
  (objects | body | numbers | whatif), hook_question (a question
  with an approved opening for the topic_family), false_belief (the wrong idea the video corrects; may be ""
  ONLY for numbers and whatif topics), closing_reframe (the sentence that
  renames the object/fact), share_archetype (the specific person the
  spoken closing names). Scene level: transformation ("start state → end
  state"), camera (how it moves; static is rejected), plus id, name,
  prompt, narration, target_duration_s, and foley (the diegetic sounds
  wanted; required for t2v/keyframes scenes). Optional: engine_override
  ("keyframes", only inside a t2v production; needs engine_override_reason),
  audio_style (legacy), caption_draft, shot_type, qa_note, data_overlays,
  visual_identity. engine_reason (one line: why this primary engine) is
  REQUIRED at production level. A route listed in
  scenes_schema.DISABLED_ROUTES is rejected as primary engine or override.
  Any other override, or any code3d scene in a non-code3d production,
  aborts before spend. Productions created before this schema (pilot v1,
  puntos-de-sutura-v2) are frozen controls and no longer pass validation.
- factory/editor.py — narration per scene (edge-tts) sets the timeline;
  clips are trimmed, slowed (max 1.25x) or last-frame-frozen to fit;
  subtitles are rendered as PNG overlays (this machine's ffmpeg has no
  libass); outputs final.mp4 (1080x1920, 30 fps, H.264/AAC) + cover.jpg.
- Audio architecture (config-driven, 3 layers): the code provides the
  mixer, the production's scenes.json "audio" block declares the recipe.
  Layers: narration (narration_volume), clip effects (effects_volume,
  used only when clip_audio is "effects"; "off" discards the clips' own
  audio) and music (music_file relative to the production folder, looped
  or trimmed to the video length, at music_volume). Clip audio goes
  through a no-music gate first: the mean volume of each clip's audio
  track is measured (volumedetect) before mixing; above
  audio.clip_gate_db (default -35 dB) it is music-level continuous
  audio and is muted entirely, at or below it is genuine foley and is
  admitted at effects_volume. Every decision (scene, measured dB,
  admitted/muted) is logged in the build summary and build/audio-gate.json.
  `editor.py <production> --gate-report` measures without rendering.
  Never retune the threshold silently: if measured values stop
  separating music from foley, report the values. A missing or null
  music_file skips the layer and says so in the run summary — never a
  failure. Different channels declare different profiles; never
  hard-code a recipe in the editor.
- Clip-audio REQUEST doctrine (standing requirement, owner 2026-10-25):
  every model-generated video request must (a) positively name the exact
  diegetic sounds wanted for that scene and (b) explicitly forbid music:
  "no music, no soundtrack, no background score". This is enforced in
  code: each t2v/keyframes scene REQUIRES a `foley` field (diegetic
  sounds only, never the word music) and the producer builds the audio
  clause from a fixed template — "Audio: the only sounds are <foley>. No
  music, no soundtrack, no background score, no voices, no narration."
  (scenes_schema.audio_clause; the old production-level audio_style is a
  legacy fallback only). The request is BEST-EFFORT, never trusted.
  Measured compliance with the earlier softer clause: v2 3 of 6 clips
  clean, v3 2 of 6. The clip-audio gate remains the enforcement
  mechanism, with a known limit: it measures mean volume of ONE mixed
  stem, so a muted clip loses its real animation foley together with the
  music, and a quiet clip it admits may still carry quiet music (the
  owner heard model music in ALL v3 clips, some also with real foley such
  as the ant biting or the wound opening — v3 ended with no animation
  sound). FUTURE WORK, not built: an Editor SFX-library layer (hand-placed
  sound effects per scene, decoupled from the video model's audio) as the
  durable fix.
- Editor v2 (reference style, built 2026-10-26; tests/test_editor_v2.py):
  * Subtitles: spoken word bold white, others grey (150,150,155), thin
    4 px black outline, no dark box; words listed in the scene field
    `subtitle_emphasis` get a thin teal selection box with corner handles
    while spoken.
  * Overlay types beyond counter/bar/label: chip (teal pill; never a
    source chip), bracket (teal, figure-less measure), ring
    (dotted teal circle), mark (red X / gold ring pop with overshoot),
    cta (acted-out phone: FOLLOW tap, share sheet, chat bubble, sent
    tick; Spanish strings come from the spec). label style "tag" is the
    tiny ALL-CAPS tracked label with an optional leader_to hairline;
    counter style "mono" with `from`; bar style "slim" (gold fill,
    optional marker, show_value false).
  * Placement and timing: x, y = graphic centre as a frame fraction;
    keyframes [{t, x, y}] move it (so a label follows an object); exactly
    one of at_offset_seconds or at_word (WORD LOCK: the overlay starts on
    that spoken word of the scene, optional offset_s, resolved from the
    narration's per-word timings). Positioned overlays may overlap in
    time; legacy centred ones may not.
  * Production field end_card {duration_s, handle, dim}: after the last
    scene the final frame is held and dimmed to `dim` brightness, a
    placeholder logo and the handle (@[CANAL] until the channel exists)
    fade in; narration/effects go silent, the music continues.
  * Not built: inline emoji in subtitles.
- Data overlays (editor): a scene may declare data_overlays, a list of
  {type: counter | bar | label, text_or_value, at_offset_seconds,
  duration_s?, visible_s?}, timed from the scene's narration start and
  placed at the frame CENTRE (the upper band belongs to the subtitles):
  a counter counts up to its number ("170 °C"), a bar fills to a fraction
  or percentage ("75%"), a label fades in. Bold white, thick black
  outline, rendered with Pillow as one PNG sequence per overlay. Overlays
  of one scene may not overlap in time. Numbers in the script should be
  carried by overlays, not only spoken.
- Visual identity (values: the dark stage in the Reference doctrine
  above; the mechanism below is unchanged): ONE `visual_identity` block per channel (background
  hex + description, palette hexes, lighting and material wording, the
  shared `unify` pass parameters, qa_tolerance_de). Each production
  embeds a verbatim copy in scenes.json so it stays reproducible. EVERY
  engine consumes it literally: the producer injects it right after the
  style string in every t2v and image prompt (same style string, same
  seed); code3d scenes use the exact hex values and render on the spec
  background. The editor applies ONE unifying pass to every scene before
  assembly, whatever its engine: shared colour grade (eq/colorbalance) +
  film grain + vignette. Engines change only at scene boundaries, never
  mid-action. QA: the backdrop and its QA apply ONLY to establishing
  (wide) shots and to code3d productions; close-ups are exempt because
  their "background" is the subject — there, consistency is carried by
  the palette, the lighting wording and the editor's unify pass. A scene
  declares shot_type "establishing" (default) or "closeup". For checked
  scenes the background colour of the raw clip is sampled from 3 frames
  (median of 8 border patches) and compared with the spec hex by CIE76
  delta-E; scenes above qa_tolerance_de (default 15) get a REVIEW mark
  in the build report and build/visual-qa.json. A mark is a signal for
  human review, never an automatic defect (an intentional background
  is recorded with the scene's qa_note, e.g. the jungle in stitches
  scene 6). Sampling is done BEFORE the vignette, which darkens the
  corners.
- Phases: A Producer ✓ · B Editor ✓ · C Scriptwriter (an LLM agent that
  writes scenes.json) · D Orchestrator · E Publisher (YouTube API,
  uploads private; the owner always makes the publish call).
- A production lives in productions/<channel-id>/<NNN>-<slug>/ (see "Repository
  organization"); scenes.json, costs.json, notes.md and publish.json are
  versioned, generated media is gitignored.

## code3d sourcing and optimization (binding, owner 2026-10-25)
Your job is the FASTEST route to each clip, not the most from-scratch one.
- (For HERO objects, "Asset library and hero routing" supersedes this list: its source order applies, and
  an AI-generated mesh needs a recorded lens decision, and a provider needing an account or credits stays owner-only. This list still applies to supporting props.)
- SOURCE FIRST: search the libraries before modelling anything; model only
  what no library has. For every element choose in this order and log a
  one-line reason: (1) an existing library asset (BlenderKit, Poly Haven,
  ambientCG, Poly Pizza), (2) an AI-generated mesh (Meshy free tier; its
  output is CC-BY and needs an attribution line), (3) modelled primitives,
  only when 1 and 2 have nothing usable.
- Free tiers only. NEVER subscribe or sign up for a paid plan (including

## Script flatness, floor handling and modellability (binding, owner 2026-11-06; counter-recommendations in docs/current/reports/resume-006-008-009-report.md)
Why: the owner watched the remade 010: more dynamism, still boring, "the whole video is a heart pumping; nothing HAPPENS". His diagnosis: the problem is the SCRIPT, not the staging.
- **Script flatness (scriptwriter lens, checked at script stage, before any production work):** a script is FLAT if its scenes are variations of a single action or state. Every script needs EVENT PROGRESSION: new elements entering, states transforming, the situation changing scene to scene, a turn or reveal. Camera movement, zooms and counters do NOT count as progression. A flat script is reshaped or rejected at script stage, exactly as a failed hero is rejected at the gate. Test: list each scene's one-line event; if scene N could be swapped with scene N+1 and nothing in the story changes, the script is flat.
- **Likeness measurement (owner 2026-11-07, counter-recommendation 1 of the resume report; supersedes pair readings everywhere, heroes and secondary elements):** THREE readers; a scene passes on a mean of at least 60 with no reader under 45. The floor itself is not lowered.
- **Flatness additions (owner 2026-11-07):** the turn comes BEFORE the closing reframe; each scene's beat sheet carries a one-line "what changes in the world".
- **Likeness-gate floor:** the standing floor (60) and the every-element rule stay unchanged for ALL future productions; a permanently lower bar would bring back the primitive shapes of the rejected batch. ONE-OFF exception, owner 2026-11-06: productions 006, 008 and 009 may declare `element_floor_exception` {floor, reason, authorised} in scenes.json, which lowers the floor for SECONDARY elements only (never `hero_ids`) and is stamped on every record it affects. It exists in code only for these three; no other production may declare it.
- **Modellability in topic selection:** when topics are proposed and judged, weigh whether EVERY element the video needs can plausibly reach hero standard through the available routes (library assets, real materials, builds). Prefer topics that pass; a topic that would foreseeably need a lowered floor gets ONE reshape attempt ("Reshape ladder", slate screening) and is dropped only if that attempt fails. **Context-cue check (owner 2026-11-07):** an element reads when its context identifies it (a tray identifies ice, a handle identifies a thermos); judge elements with their context, not as isolated objects. The check is recorded next to each topic proposal.

## Topic selection: curiosity test and one-line hook test (binding, owner direction 2026-11-07; marketing lens owns topics)
Why: by the owner's read the recent topics are well made but too neat ("school-science facts"); a stranger is not curious enough to keep watching. His read of the catalogue is that 005 ("where does the poop go") is the best performer so far: an everyday, slightly gross question everyone has wondered about, with a hidden journey behind it. (No platform analytics are in the repo; this is the owner's read, not a measurement.)
- **CURIOSITY TEST:** propose only topics that name a question the viewer ALREADY carries (something they have wondered, been told, or done), ideally with a tense side: gross, hidden, bodily, dangerous or enormous. Neat facts nobody asked about fail. Not every topic must be polemical; every topic must earn a stranger's curiosity in one line. Each proposal carries a curiosity score (1-10, marketing judgement, stated as judgement) with a one-line reason.
- **ONE-LINE HOOK TEST:** write the hook (approved opening + explicit false belief) at proposal stage. If it cannot be written strongly, the topic dies there, before any script work.

A high-curiosity topic that fails modellability gets one reshape attempt ("Reshape ladder") before it may be dropped for modellability, and the slate says which. Gross stays conceptual: stylised, no real skin texture, no blood, no gore.
- **TOPIC BALANCE (owner 2026-11-07, second direction of the day):** 005 was ONE example of a tense, gross topic that worked, not an instruction to pick toilet or poop topics. The curiosity axis is tension IN GENERAL (gross, hidden, bodily, dangerous, enormous), and a slate stays balanced across it. Every topic carries a theme tag and a main-tension tag. Lens criterion (2026-11-07, logged; restated for bank draws the same day): in ANY five consecutive productions of a channel, at most two share a theme, and at least three of the five tensions appear; a draw of any size is checked against that rolling window together with the productions before it. Transitional: the 2026-11-07 slate sits at the ceiling on the toilet theme (012, 016), so the first draw after it takes no toilet/poop topic, and the wet-wipes topic has lost its "first in line" status. Balance never overrides the curiosity test: it chooses among topics that pass it. **Theme tags name the SPECIFIC SUBJECT (owner ruling 2026-11-07):** a theme tag names what the topic is about (toilet, electricity, elevators), never a broad everyday setting it happens to use (car, kitchen, street, house, plane). Topics that merely share a setting do not count toward the same theme cap; topics about the same subject do, whatever their setting.
- **FAMILY MIX IS AN OUTCOME, NEVER A TARGET (owner ruling 2026-11-07):** topic-family proportions (the brand package's `topic_families` percentages) describe the portfolio; they are not a quota. No fill, slate or draw promotes a topic, generates for quota, holds a topic back, or recasts a topic's family to move the proportions, and a batch with no topic from some family is acceptable as it stands. ## Idea bank (binding, owner 2026-11-07; the standard way topics are supplied)
Why: a short owner instruction ("produce the next N videos") must inherit the whole topic logic from doctrine. Topic generation and production are separated: an Opus session fills a ranked pool, production sessions draw from it and never invent topics.
- **The bank:** `docs/current/idea-bank.md`, a standing file with TWO TIERS (owner 2026-11-07, second addition) plus the record of discards and modellability rejections. **BANKED tier:** ranked, drawable, production-ready topics. **RESERVE tier:** NOT drawable; entries that passed every screen (preliminary screen, concept-level ladder check, standing screens) but ranked below the banked cut at their fill. Reserve entries keep their full entry fields and are RE-SCORED at every later fill: an old score is a starting point, never a fossil. Banking is a topic decision under "Governance": each fill is approved by the involved lenses (marketing lead) and gets a row in `docs/current/reports/lens-decisions.md`.
- **The standard fill cycle (an Opus session), whenever the owner asks to bank N topics; also the refill:**
  1. *READ FIRST,* before generating anything: the idea bank (both tiers and its discard record), the channel index `productions/mecanalia-es/INDEX.md` (produced topics), and the discards and rejections recorded by earlier fills and slates (from 2026-11-07: `docs/current/reports/topic-slate-2026-11-07.md`). Generation must not re-propose a topic already banked, reserved, produced or recorded as discarded: impossible-class discards and ladder-check failures keep their reasons until a capability change, and if the session believes one has revived a discard, it FLAGS that in its report instead of silently re-proposing it. **HELD FOR BALANCE is not discarded (owner amendment 2026-11-07):** a candidate rejected ONLY to keep a batch or slate balanced, recorded as such (first case: wet wipes and fatbergs, `topic-slate-2026-11-07.md` row 4), is not blocked by this rule; it is listed in the bank's held-for-balance record and re-enters the pool automatically (step 6) at the first fill where TOPIC BALANCE allows its theme again, judged with the same test as the cut (step 7). It is then re-scored like a reserve entry.
  2. *Generate wide,* at least 2N fresh candidates (for N = 10, at least twenty), on curiosity: the CURIOSITY TEST and the ONE-LINE HOOK TEST above (a topic whose hook cannot be written strongly dies here). At generation only the hard production rules (no gore, no blood) filter.
  3. *Preliminary screen:* discard ONLY the known-impossible classes, each discard recorded with its class: invisible protagonist (nothing on screen can carry the mechanism), banned content (the hard rules), subjects already recorded as gate failures with no conceivable reshape.
  4. *Ladder check, one level deep, at concept level* (this is the "Reshape ladder" slate-screening attempt): modellable as conceived, or modellable with ONE reshape (beats re-staged or rewritten, narration adjusted), judged conceptually with the context-cue check, no asset searches. An entry carries its concept-level reshape if it needed one; a topic is rejected for modellability only after this check fails, with the attempt recorded. The check may be run on the strongest survivors only, but only a checked survivor enters the pool; an unchecked one is recorded as not pooled (it is not a discard).
  5. *Entry fields* (banked and reserve alike): hook (question with an approved opening + false belief), topic family, structure variant, curiosity score (judgement, with its one-line reason), hero and a one-line route note, any concept-level reshape applied, theme tag and main-tension tag (for TOPIC BALANCE). The standing screens (pan-Hispanic and universal, on the channel's promise, format policy) are applied before an entry enters the pool, since a pooled entry must be production-ready; script flatness is checked when the production session writes the script.
  6. *Pool and rank:* the pool is the fresh survivors PLUS every current reserve entry (re-scored) PLUS every held-for-balance candidate whose theme balance now allows (re-scored). Rank the pool together on ONE rubric: curiosity score, the lenses breaking ties on modellability confidence (as conceived above reshaped).
  7. *Cut, balance-aware (owner amendment 2026-11-07):* fill the banked tier from the top of the pooled ranking until it holds N drawable entries, but SKIP a candidate that would leave the banked tier unable to satisfy TOPIC BALANCE across its next draws, judged against the most recent productions (the rolling window) and the tier's own composition, in favour of the next candidate that keeps the tier drawable. Rank governs inside that constraint. A skipped candidate goes to the reserve tier with the reason "balance at the cut" recorded; as a reserve entry it is re-pooled at every fill. Existing undrawn banked entries count toward N and are never demoted by a fill (they may be re-scored on the same rubric, which can reorder the banked tier but never moves an entry to the reserve). A reserve entry that makes the cut is PROMOTED into the banked tier. Pool entries that miss the cut, fresh or previously reserved, sit in the reserve tier. Preliminary-screen discards and ladder-check failures stay out of both tiers, recorded with their reasons.
  8. *Depth:* the banked tier's working depth is at least TEN undrawn entries. A refill is due when a draw would leave fewer; a fill asked for N below ten is done as asked and its report says the bank sits below working depth. Generation aims wide across themes and tensions so that the balance-aware cut (step 7) can fill N without running short; if it cannot, the report says how many drawable entries balance allowed.
  9. *Commissioning scan:* the fill ends with the scan in asset-system.md "Advance assets" (e), opening the job of every element class that qualifies.
- **Drawing (a production session; Sonnet):** for "produce N videos", take the N highest-ranked undrawn entries of the BANKED tier (never the reserve) that keep the rolling window inside TOPIC BALANCE (skip, never reorder the ranking for any other reason), mark them drawn in the bank (production number and date), and produce them under standing doctrine, applying the "Reshape ladder" live whenever a scene complicates. If fewer than N entries are eligible, produce the eligible ones and report the shortfall; NEVER fill the gap with an unbanked topic. If a draw leaves fewer than ten undrawn banked entries, the report says a refill is due (the production session does not refill). A drawn topic that fails beyond the ladder (it would need a new hero mechanism or promise, or fails script flatness and cannot be reshaped under "Script flatness") is marked returned in the bank with its reason, and the session may draw the next eligible banked entry in its place.

## Reshape ladder (binding, owner 2026-11-07; integration recorded in docs/current/reports/lens-decisions.md)
Why: scenes that were hard to model or failed the likeness gate have stalled whole productions (009 stays skipped on one scene), and topics have been dropped for capability limits. A capability limit is a problem for the staging or the beat, not a reason to stall or abandon the topic.
- **Trigger:** a scene proves hard to model (the house-rule timebox: past 60 min or 2x its estimate) or a declared element fails the likeness gate. The production does not stall: the other scenes continue, and the failing scene works the ladder IN ORDER:
  1. **RE-STAGE the beat:** same script and narration, different staging (another framing, another context cue, another view or cutaway of the same element, another library asset or route).
  2. **REWRITE THE BEAT** in the script to a modellable equivalent that does the SAME narrative job (same fact or step of the mechanism, same position in the event progression, same turn if it is the turn).
  3. **ADJUST THE NARRATION** line to match the new beat.
  4. **CONTEXT STAGING (owner 2026-11-10; final rung, element-level):** re-stage the failing ELEMENT embedded in its narrative setting, so the setting visible in the frame identifies the element's situation (for example a swallowed object already inside a modelled digestive tract instead of the object alone). The gate reads the element from that staging under the unchanged rules. Owner rules: (1) the context must be visible inside the rendered frame; the blind readers' isolation does not change (they see only neutrally named stills, no narration, title or topic information); (2) the pass bar does not move: every reader names the element by one of its declared aliases, mean confidence >= 60, no reader under 45; (3) it applies ONLY to scene elements inside a production: library asset blind naming (`asset_library.py blind` / `record_blind`) stays isolated forever, with no context staging for sealing an asset; (4) ONE attempt per element, attempted after the re-staging rungs and before the topic is returned; if it also fails, the ladder is exhausted and the topic is returned as today; (5) a pass through this rung is flagged in the production's notes.md (which element, which scene, that it passed via context staging) so the owner's verdicts on such videos can be reviewed against it later. Doctrine only: no gate, runner or verifier code changed.
  Each rung gets a real attempt before the next; a further failed gate round on the same rung is the signal to step down, not to iterate the same staging indefinitely. The timebox is still reported.
- **Where the ladder stops:** before replacing the HERO MECHANISM or the topic's PROMISE (the hook question and its false belief, and what the video answers). A fix that needs either is a different video and goes back to the slate.
- **Not capability limits, never laddered around:** the hard production rules (no gore, no blood, no real skin texture, stylised). A beat or topic blocked by one of them is not reshaped to get past it; it is dropped or re-proposed on its own merits.
- **Every rung keeps every standing bar:** the likeness gate at full strength (three readers, mean >= 60, none under 45; never an added alias, never a lowered floor; the one-off `element_floor_exception` of 006/008/009 does not extend); the every-element routing, so no flat or abstract stand-in replaces an element that failed (the drawability rule: without a passing asset it stays off screen); the dynamism bar; script flatness (a rewritten beat still changes the world, the scene-swap test still fails, the turn still comes before the closing reframe, the beat sheet's "what changes in the world" line is updated); the format policy (the production's `structure_variant`, hook formula and closing slot are unchanged; scenes are never merged or padded to absorb a reshape; if the scene count changes, the rotation rule is re-checked); and, after rung 3, the duration band, word locks and sourced numbers. A rung that cannot keep these fails, and the next rung is tried.
- **Approval and record:** rungs 2 and 3 change the approved beat sheet, so the involved lenses re-approve it under "Governance" (scriptwriter lens leads). Every reshape is logged in the production's notes.md AND as a row in `docs/current/reports/lens-decisions.md`: what changed, which rung, why (the failing measurement or timebox).
- **At slate screening, one attempt deep:** a topic that fails modellability as conceived gets ONE reshape attempt (rung 1 or 2 applied to the failing beat on paper, with the context-cue check, rung 3 as needed; no asset searches) before it may be rejected for modellability. Since the IDEA BANK, this is step 3 of a bank fill; the bank records the attempt and its outcome, and a banked entry carries its concept-level reshape. At production time the ladder starts again from rung 1 on the live scene. Rejections for the hard rules, the curiosity test, the hook test or balance are not modellability rejections and get no ladder attempt.
- **Relation to script flatness:** a flat script is a script defect, not a capability limit; it is reshaped or rejected under "Script flatness". The ladder never makes a passing script flat.

## Dynamism is a hard bar (binding, owner 2026-11-05; supersedes any reading of "transformation" and "camera" as paperwork)
Why: production 010 (heart and bottles) was well modelled but held ONE static framing for the whole video while a counter climbed; monotony has been a rejection cause before (the original 006/007 "the same scene throughout", 010 now).
- The per-scene `transformation` and `camera` fields are a bar the render must meet, not a description. **No video may hold one static tableau while only a counter or overlay changes.** Every scene stages a visible change in the world (an object transforms, grows, multiplies, moves through states) AND a camera that travels (not a hold, and not a push-in on a still action).
- **Escalation is staged as movement the viewer travels through:** a scale, count or growth ("a thousand bottles", "seven thousand litres") is shown as physical growth and camera travel (the pile builds, the camera climbs or pulls back along it, framings change from close to wide), never as a number rising in a fixed frame. Numbers stay overlays, in addition.
- **Across a video, framings vary:** no two consecutive scenes may share the same camera move and distance; at least three distinct framings per video; the mechanism (what the thing does) is shown at work, not only its parts at rest.
- Checked by the lenses before a final render (contact sheet of each scene's first, middle and last frame: if the three frames of a scene are the same picture, the scene is rejected) and audited in every production report. Applies to every future production; productions made before this rule are audited, not reworked, unless the owner asks.

## Support colours and composition gate (binding, owner 2026-10-28)
The stage colour is the BACKGROUND ONLY. Every support element (backing boards, cutaway plates, plinths, panels, large housing surfaces) gets a colour chosen per production by the design lens:
1. SEPARATION: CIEDE2000 >= 20 from BOTH ends of the stage gradient (`identity.support_errors`).
2. HARMONY: derived from the hero, ink or accent system; value and reason recorded in the production's notes.md.
Support colours are parameters (`identity.<preset>.support {board, plate, panel}`, overridden per production by `scenes.json` `support_colours`; per-production hero colours in `hero_colours`), never code literals and never the stage value.
THE GATE IS A HARD BLOCK in the render driver: `factory/code3d_render.py --mode final` refuses to launch (exit 2) unless the production has a FRESH PASSING gate record (`build/gate/gate.json` + the distance table `build/gate/gate.md` + the stills). Create it with
`.venv/bin/python factory/gate.py run productions/<ch>/<video>` (renders the stills declared in scenes.json `gate_stills` {scene id: [frames]} with `mask=1` under caffeinate, tabulates the pairs through `tools/composition_gate.py`); `gate.py check` verifies it. The record carries a fingerprint of the identity preset, support/hero colours, the gate scene scripts and the rig libraries (scenes.json `rig_libs`), so any change makes it stale and forces a re-run.
Thresholds: stage-support, stage-hero, support-hero >= 20 (CIEDE2000, in frame); accent-stage >= 20 and 3:1 luminance contrast; the rendered accent/error must match its swatch family (dE <= 15; calibrate the preset's `emission_gain`). Any BLOCK stops the render until fixed.

## Topic selection: curiosity test and one-line hook test (binding, owner direction 2026-11-07; marketing lens owns topics)
Why: by the owner's read the recent topics are well made but too neat ("school-science facts"); a stranger is not curious enough to keep watching. His read of the catalogue is that 005 ("where does the poop go") is the best performer so far: an everyday, slightly gross question everyone has wondered about, with a hidden journey behind it. (No platform analytics are in the repo; this is the owner's read, not a measurement.)
- **CURIOSITY TEST:** propose only topics that name a question the viewer ALREADY carries (something they have wondered, been told, or done), ideally with a tense side: gross, hidden, bodily, dangerous or enormous. Neat facts nobody asked about fail. Not every topic must be polemical; every topic must earn a stranger's curiosity in one line. Each proposal carries a curiosity score (1-10, marketing judgement, stated as judgement) with a one-line reason.
- **ONE-LINE HOOK TEST:** write the hook (approved opening + explicit false belief) at proposal stage. If it cannot be written strongly, the topic dies there, before any script work.
- Both tests sit ON TOP of the standing screens, never instead of them: universal pan-Hispanic topics, format policy, modellability and context-cue check, script flatness, the closing standard. A high-curiosity topic that fails modellability gets one reshape attempt ("Reshape ladder") before it may be dropped for modellability, and the slate says which. Gross stays conceptual: stylised, no real skin texture, no blood, no gore.
- **TOPIC BALANCE (owner 2026-11-07, second direction of the day):** 005 was ONE example of a tense, gross topic that worked, not an instruction to pick toilet or poop topics. The curiosity axis is tension IN GENERAL (gross, hidden, bodily, dangerous, enormous), and a slate stays balanced across it. Every topic carries a theme tag and a main-tension tag. Lens criterion (2026-11-07, logged; restated for bank draws the same day): in ANY five consecutive productions of a channel, at most two share a theme, and at least three of the five tensions appear; a draw of any size is checked against that rolling window together with the productions before it. Balance never overrides the curiosity test: it chooses among topics that pass it. **Theme tags name the SPECIFIC SUBJECT (owner ruling 2026-11-07):** a theme tag names what the topic is about (toilet, electricity, elevators), never a broad everyday setting it happens to use (car, kitchen, street, house, plane). Topics that merely share a setting do not count toward the same theme cap; topics about the same subject do, whatever their setting.
- **FAMILY MIX IS AN OUTCOME, NEVER A TARGET (owner ruling 2026-11-07):** topic-family proportions (the brand package's `topic_families` percentages) describe the portfolio; they are not a quota. No fill, slate or draw promotes a topic, generates for quota, holds a topic back, or recasts a topic's family to move the proportions, and a batch with no topic from some family is acceptable as it stands. A topic's family is whatever fits its hook and structure.
- First slate under this rule: `docs/current/reports/topic-slate-2026-11-07.md` (a dated record; the productions it supplied predate the IDEA BANK). Every topic now comes from the bank.

## Idea bank (binding, owner 2026-11-07; the standard way topics are supplied)
Why: a short owner instruction ("produce the next N videos") must inherit the whole topic logic from doctrine. Topic generation and production are separated: an Opus session fills a ranked pool, a creative (Opus) session draws from it and develops each topic into a stamped script, and production sessions build those developed units and never invent topics or write scripts ("Drawing", "Who writes the script", amended 2026-11-11).
- **The bank:** `docs/current/idea-bank.md`, a standing file with TWO TIERS (owner 2026-11-07, second addition) plus the record of discards and modellability rejections. **BANKED tier:** ranked, drawable, production-ready topics. **RESERVE tier:** NOT drawable; entries that passed every screen (preliminary screen, concept-level ladder check, standing screens) but ranked below the banked cut at their fill. Reserve entries keep their full entry fields and are RE-SCORED at every later fill: an old score is a starting point, never a fossil. Banking is a topic decision under "Governance": each fill is approved by the involved lenses (marketing lead) and gets a row in `docs/current/reports/lens-decisions.md`.
- **The standard fill cycle (an Opus session), whenever the owner asks to bank N topics; also the refill:**
  1. *READ FIRST,* before generating anything: the idea bank (both tiers and its discard record), the channel index `productions/mecanalia-es/INDEX.md` (produced topics), and the discards and rejections recorded by earlier fills and slates (from 2026-11-07: `docs/current/reports/topic-slate-2026-11-07.md`). Generation must not re-propose a topic already banked, reserved, produced or recorded as discarded: impossible-class discards and ladder-check failures keep their reasons until a capability change, and if the session believes one has revived a discard, it FLAGS that in its report instead of silently re-proposing it. **HELD FOR BALANCE is not discarded (owner amendment 2026-11-07):** a candidate rejected ONLY to keep a batch or slate balanced, recorded as such (first case: wet wipes and fatbergs, `topic-slate-2026-11-07.md` row 4), is not blocked by this rule; it is listed in the bank's held-for-balance record and re-enters the pool automatically (step 6) at the first fill where TOPIC BALANCE allows its theme again, judged with the same test as the cut (step 7). It is then re-scored like a reserve entry.
  2. *Generate wide,* at least 2N fresh candidates (for N = 10, at least twenty), on curiosity: the CURIOSITY TEST and the ONE-LINE HOOK TEST above (a topic whose hook cannot be written strongly dies here). At generation only the hard production rules (no gore, no blood) filter.
  3. *Preliminary screen:* discard ONLY the known-impossible classes, each discard recorded with its class: invisible protagonist (nothing on screen can carry the mechanism), banned content (the hard rules), subjects already recorded as gate failures with no conceivable reshape.
  4. *Ladder check, one level deep, at concept level* (this is the "Reshape ladder" slate-screening attempt): modellable as conceived, or modellable with ONE reshape (beats re-staged or rewritten, narration adjusted), judged conceptually with the context-cue check, no asset searches. An entry carries its concept-level reshape if it needed one; a topic is rejected for modellability only after this check fails, with the attempt recorded. The check may be run on the strongest survivors only, but only a checked survivor enters the pool; an unchecked one is recorded as not pooled (it is not a discard).
  5. *Entry fields* (banked and reserve alike): hook (question with an approved opening + false belief), topic family, structure variant, curiosity score (judgement, with its one-line reason), hero and a one-line route note, any concept-level reshape applied, theme tag and main-tension tag (for TOPIC BALANCE). The standing screens (pan-Hispanic and universal, on the channel's promise, format policy) are applied before an entry enters the pool, since a pooled entry must be production-ready; script flatness is checked when the script is written, by the creative session that writes it ("Who writes the script" below).
  6. *Pool and rank:* the pool is the fresh survivors PLUS every current reserve entry (re-scored) PLUS every held-for-balance candidate whose theme balance now allows (re-scored). Rank the pool together on ONE rubric: curiosity score, the lenses breaking ties on modellability confidence (as conceived above reshaped).
  7. *Cut, balance-aware (owner amendment 2026-11-07):* fill the banked tier from the top of the pooled ranking until it holds N drawable entries, but SKIP a candidate that would leave the banked tier unable to satisfy TOPIC BALANCE across its next draws, judged against the most recent productions (the rolling window) and the tier's own composition, in favour of the next candidate that keeps the tier drawable. Rank governs inside that constraint. A skipped candidate goes to the reserve tier with the reason "balance at the cut" recorded; as a reserve entry it is re-pooled at every fill. Existing undrawn banked entries count toward N and are never demoted by a fill (they may be re-scored on the same rubric, which can reorder the banked tier but never moves an entry to the reserve). A reserve entry that makes the cut is PROMOTED into the banked tier. Pool entries that miss the cut, fresh or previously reserved, sit in the reserve tier. Preliminary-screen discards and ladder-check failures stay out of both tiers, recorded with their reasons.
  8. *Depth:* the banked tier's working depth is at least TEN undrawn entries. A refill is due when a draw would leave fewer; a fill asked for N below ten is done as asked and its report says the bank sits below working depth. Generation aims wide across themes and tensions so that the balance-aware cut (step 7) can fill N without running short; if it cannot, the report says how many drawable entries balance allowed.
  9. *Commissioning scan:* the fill ends with the scan in asset-system.md "Advance assets" (e), opening the job of every element class that qualifies.
- **Drawing (amended 2026-11-11, block B2; a creative session draws, a production session takes developed units):** to develop N topics, a creative (Opus) session takes the N highest-ranked undrawn entries of the BANKED tier (never the reserve) that keep the rolling window inside TOPIC BALANCE (skip, never reorder the ranking for any other reason), marks them drawn in the bank (production number and date), and develops each into a stamped script ("Who writes the script"). A production session (Sonnet; `tools/run_productions.py` pins the model) never draws from the raw bank: it produces a DEVELOPED unit (a developed-set folder in its batch, a stamped standby, or a stamped revived entry) under standing doctrine, applying the "Reshape ladder" live whenever a scene complicates. If fewer than N entries are eligible, develop the eligible ones and report the shortfall; NEVER fill the gap with an unbanked topic. If a draw leaves fewer than ten undrawn banked entries, the report says a refill is due (a production session does not refill). A developed topic that fails beyond the ladder (it would need a new hero mechanism or promise, or fails script flatness and cannot be reshaped under "Script flatness") is returned with `tools/return_topic.py`, which marks it returned in the bank with its reason and draws its replacement only from the stamped standbys ("Developed standby buffer"); with none eligible, no folder is created and the replacement waits for a creative session.
- **Who writes the script (lens decision 2026-11-11, amended the same day by block B2 on the owner's rule that Opus writes every script; canonical home of this rule; the produce skill's steps 1 and 8 point here):** EVERY script is written by an Opus creative session; a production session never writes one. The creative session writes the complete script (checked under "Script flatness" and the format policy), the audit and the per-element fallbacks (next bullet), then stamps the production folder with `.venv/bin/python factory/provenance.py stamp <prod> --model <its model id> --record "<report or lens-decision row>"` (`provenance.json`: writer, model, record, hash of script.json). A production proceeds from the stamped script and changes it only through the "Reshape ladder" (rungs 2-3, logged). An entry restored by a revival review (status `revived`, drawn by hand into its existing folder) keeps its re-checked script only when the folder carries a stamp proven by the repo's records; otherwise a creative session re-checks and stamps it. **Enforcement:** `tools/run_productions.py` refuses an unstamped unit before any session and moves on; `scenes_schema.py` (folder check), `code3d_render.py --mode final` and the producer refuse an unstamped production numbered 025 or later (`factory/provenance.py` PROVENANCE_FROM); runner sessions are denied writing or editing `provenance.json` and running the stamp command. A session that returns a topic never writes its replacement's script.
- **Per-element fallbacks (block B2, P9):** for every scene element that is not a sealed asset in a sealed pose and not a staging already recorded as passed (`assets/patterns.md`), the creative session writes the ladder fallbacks into the production's notes.md before the production starts: the rung-1 re-staging(s), the rung-2 beat rewrite with its rung-3 narration line, and the rung-4 context staging. Where a blind proof of the element exists, the fallbacks are designed from its actual readings. The production tries the written fallbacks first, rung by rung, within the reading budget ("Reshape ladder"); it may still work a rung with its own staging when the written one fails.
- **Developed standby buffer (block B2):** a creative session keeps developed topics in reserve for returns: bank status `standby <NNN> <date>` (folder NNN created with `tools/new_production.py`, stamped script on file, in no batch). Working depth: at least the number of returns the next batch is expected to produce at the latest measured return rate, rounded up. A standby joins a batch by changing its status to `drawn <NNN> <date> (developed set; script on file)`. `tools/return_topic.py` draws the highest-ranked stamped standby whose blind proofs have passed (next bullet) and that keeps TOPIC BALANCE, and marks it `drawn <NNN> <date> (developed standby; replacement for returned <MMM>)`; with none, it creates no folder, records the replacement as pending a creative session, and the runner logs it and moves on.

- **Blind proofs before batch eligibility (lens decision 2026-11-11, block B4, P8):** the creative session that develops a unit writes `<prod>/proofs.json` with one proof for every element that is not a sealed asset in a sealed pose and not a staging recorded as passed in `assets/patterns.md` (the scope of "Per-element fallbacks"), one asset seal for every declared library asset that is not `ready`, and a render-cost measurement when the unit is a production's first `world` environment. Each element proof names the element, its kind, its aliases exactly as the script declares them, the staging of the still, the reuse it builds from, and what the creative session redesigns if it fails. A braked proof unit (Sonnet) builds each still and reads it with `tools/blind_read.py` under the unchanged bar (three isolated readers, mean >= 60, none under 45, no alias added); Opus sessions never build proofs. Every proof reading is recorded in `proofs.json` and counts; it is a pre-production measurement, not a ladder rung, and does not spend the production's reading budget. A failed proof goes back to a creative session, which redesigns from the actual readings (a new staging, new written fallbacks, or the topic back to the bank). **A unit joins a batch only when every proof has passed:** `tools/run_productions.py` refuses a unit whose `proofs.json` holds a proof not `passed`, and `tools/return_topic.py` never draws such a standby (`factory/proofs.py`). A folder without `proofs.json` predates this rule and is not refused by it.

## Script flatness, floor handling and modellability (binding, owner 2026-11-06; counter-recommendations in docs/current/reports/resume-006-008-009-report.md)
Why: the owner watched the remade 010: more dynamism, still boring, "the whole video is a heart pumping; nothing HAPPENS". His diagnosis: the problem is the SCRIPT, not the staging.
- **Script flatness (scriptwriter lens, checked at script stage, before any production work):** a script is FLAT if its scenes are variations of a single action or state. Every script needs EVENT PROGRESSION: new elements entering, states transforming, the situation changing scene to scene, a turn or reveal. Camera movement, zooms and counters do NOT count as progression. A flat script is reshaped or rejected at script stage, exactly as a failed hero is rejected at the gate. Test: list each scene's one-line event; if scene N could be swapped with scene N+1 and nothing in the story changes, the script is flat.
- **Likeness measurement (owner 2026-11-07, counter-recommendation 1 of the resume report; supersedes pair readings everywhere, heroes and secondary elements):** THREE readers; a scene passes on a mean of at least 60 with no reader under 45. The floor itself is not lowered.
- **Flatness additions (owner 2026-11-07):** the turn comes BEFORE the closing reframe; each scene's beat sheet carries a one-line "what changes in the world".
- **Likeness-gate floor:** the standing floor (60) and the every-element rule stay unchanged for ALL future productions; a permanently lower bar would bring back the primitive shapes of the rejected batch. ONE-OFF exception, owner 2026-11-06: productions 006, 008 and 009 may declare `element_floor_exception` {floor, reason, authorised} in scenes.json, which lowers the floor for SECONDARY elements only (never `hero_ids`) and is stamped on every record it affects. It exists in code only for these three; no other production may declare it.
- **Modellability in topic selection:** when topics are proposed and judged, weigh whether EVERY element the video needs can plausibly reach hero standard through the available routes (library assets, real materials, builds). Prefer topics that pass; a topic that would foreseeably need a lowered floor gets ONE reshape attempt ("Reshape ladder", slate screening) and is dropped only if that attempt fails. **Context-cue check (owner 2026-11-07):** an element reads when its context identifies it (a tray identifies ice, a handle identifies a thermos); judge elements with their context, not as isolated objects. The check is recorded next to each topic proposal.

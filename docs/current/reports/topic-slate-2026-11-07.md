# Topic slate, 2026-11-07: the first slate under the curiosity test

**Owner direction (2026-11-07):** recent topics read as boring. They are well made, but they do not make a stranger curious. By the owner's read, 005 ("where does the poop go") is the best performer so far. Take more risk in topic choice, and prefer tension (gross, hidden, bodily, dangerous, enormous) to neat school-science facts. The direction is recorded as doctrine in CLAUDE.md "Topic selection" and in `lens-decisions.md`.

**How the lenses ran:** one session reasoned through each lens under the multi-lens protocol: marketing led, with scriptwriter and design consulted. They were not separate agents. No platform analytics exist in the repo, so curiosity scores are marketing judgement and not measurements.

## The screens, in order
1. **Curiosity test.** Does the topic name a question the viewer already carries? Does it have a tense side? The score runs 1-10.
2. **One-line hook test.** Write the hook with an approved opening and an explicit false belief. If it is weak, the topic dies here.
3. **Modellability and context cue.** Every element the video needs must plausibly reach hero standard through the routes we have (library assets, real materials, builds), judged in context. A topic whose protagonist is invisible or microscopic (a gas, a molecule, a microbe) fails, because no route makes it read. The same goes for a topic that needs an element that has already failed the gate, such as vessels inside a fingertip cutaway.
4. **Standing doctrine.** The topic must be universal across the Spanish-speaking world. Gross stays conceptual: no blood, no gore, no real skin texture. The script must not be flat.
5. **Batch balance.** The family mix is objects 40 / body 25 / numbers 20 / whatif 15. Rotation forbids three videos in a row with the same variant or the same scene count. No single theme should dominate the batch.

## Lens positions
- **Marketing (lead):** the five are all questions a stranger already carries, and four of the five have a gross side. 016 is gross and enormous, the owner's exact steer. Every closing archetype is a recognisable behaviour (the brush by the toilet, cracking before typing, swallowing gum to talk, the phone in the bathroom).
- **Scriptwriter:** every script passes the flatness test. Two use a named-person or real-case turn (Unger in 013, the Milov children in 014), which also satisfies storytelling rule 7.
- **Design:** no topic needs an element that has already failed the gate, except the finger cutaway in 013. That cutaway is risky, but the bones give it a context cue, so it is measured first. 012 reuses the toilet from 005 (an accepted control). 013 reuses the hand (85/85). The unknowns are searches, not builds: the digestive-system asset (014), the housefly and its macro detail (015), and the horse and cars (016).
- **Disagreement settled:** marketing ranked mosquitoes (10) above two of the chosen five. Design blocked it on written doctrine (the no-blood rule, and an element with a measured failure), so it lost by the rule, not by argument.

## Open points (lens matter, not owner matter; nothing changed)
- **"¿Adónde…?" opening.** 005's hook ("¿Adónde va la caca…?") is not in today's approved openings, which are "¿Por qué / ¿Cuántos / ¿Qué pasaría si…". The owner's read of 005 is one judgement and not a measurement, so the doctrine stands. All five hooks use approved openings. If 005's retention data, once available, beats the "¿Por qué" videos, the lenses should test adding "¿Adónde…" for `journey`.
- **Flagged figures** to confirm before each beat sheet is final (listed in each `notes.md`): the Gerba 1975 droplet bacteria (012), Unsworth 1971 (013, the ~20-minute figure itself is verified), standard gum and corn digestion physiology (014), fly taste-on-feet and feeding physiology (015), and the comparison weights (016).

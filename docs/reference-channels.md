## @unminutodeporques — findings

Format: polished 3D renders, Spanish voiceover, subtitles in the TOP band,
current word marked by a dark rounded box. Durations ~36–75 s.

Performance (likes):
- HITS: gears/gear ratios 143K · chess possible games 86K · Rubik's cube
  permutations 50K · folding paper to the Moon 49K · cure for viruses 47K ·
  relationships 73K (off-format meme-joke video)
- FLOPS: onion layers 360 · microwave heating 590 · rice-and-the-king
  legend 5K · falling into Jupiter 335

Lessons:
- The top family is IMPOSSIBLE NUMBERS / SCALE: chess game count, Rubik
  permutations, gear ratios, paper folding. The spectacle is multiplication:
  objects multiply, counters and bars grow on screen, synced to the words.
- Dynamism is INSIDE the shot: transformation arcs (flat gear train →
  planetary system), continuous camera travel, objects assembling. It is NOT
  fast cutting — hits run ~7–15 s per setup, similar to ordinary explainers.
- The flops are FLAT 2D diagram videos of textbook topics (onion, microwave)
  with no transformation and no number spectacle.
- Top-band subtitles are a house constant, including in flop videos — the
  position is part of the recognisable format, not a performance variable.

## @resultaaquee — findings

Format: polished 3D, one HERO OBJECT per video (phone, tape measure) orbited
continuously, objects turning transparent / x-ray / cross-section with
diagram labels, spoken closing slot. Durations ~55–95 s, ~4–7 setups.
Subtitles in the top band, current word in a dark rounded box.

Performance (likes / comments):
- HITS: touchscreen 753K · tape measure (the hook) 314K · iPhone keyboard
  writing zones 244K / 9,952 comments · virus cure ~236K across accounts
- COMMENT MAGNETS: baby-Hitler dilemma 9.2K likes / 441 comments (flat 2D
  style — the question carries it); touchscreen comments full of viewers
  reporting they TESTED the claim on their own phone.

Lessons:
- Topics the viewer can VERIFY OR TRY ON THE SPOT explode in comments
  (touch a screen edge, type on the keyboard zones, look at a tape measure).
- Closing is a fixed spoken slot: follow + "send this to that specific
  friend who…" naming a concrete person archetype per video. No end card.
  Dilemma videos repeat the opening question at the end.
- Audio is voice-led: fast continuous narration, no prominent music bed.
- Their own meta-video states the engine: "you don't edit, you write — you
  draw with code." Claude + a rules folder (SKILL.md), a cheap TTS voice,
  each word drawn as a 3D animation synced to the audio at 60 fps, every
  frame reviewed. Their weak spot, same evidence: ORGANIC/human topics —
  the dilemma video degraded to flat 2D and underperformed. Code-drawn 3D
  is strong for rigid geometric objects, weak for bodies and characters.
- Captions are minimal (hook line + funnel CTA).

## @ingenioculto — findings

Format: polished 3D renders, ONE fast male Spanish voice, NO prominent music
or SFX, subtitles upper-centre advancing word-by-word, current word usually
in a dark pill (one reel emphasised by bright-white-vs-grey instead). ~45–55 s.
Distinctive: the topic is LOCALIZED to Mexico ("curiosities of what you see
and use every day" — in your country) and the CAPTIONS are long mini-articles
that contain the hook and the twist, not just a funnel CTA.

Performance (likes):
- HITS: why new tires have little hairs 118K · pregnancy test (the second
  line is real gold nanoparticles) 94K · why the Mexico City Metro runs on
  rubber tires 73K · escalator brush that cleans nothing 61K · hole in plug
  prongs 45K · rooftop water tank (tinaco) 37K · air-traffic holding patterns
  20K
- FLOPS: tornado formation (generic science) 302 · phone GPS by satellite
  (generic science) 360 · boss messaging after hours / labour law 500 ·
  Santos soccer winning streak (sports news) 18K

Format comparison of the inspected hits vs flops (IMPORTANT — the surface
template is the same in both groups, so the template alone is not the
differentiator):
1. HOOK FORMULA: hits open with a "¿Por qué…?" question about a TANGIBLE
   EVERYDAY OBJECT the viewer has already seen, plus an explicit FALSE
   BELIEF to correct ("they are not for grip"; "it is not custom or
   decoration"). Flops open with "¿Cómo…?", a scenario question
   ("¿Debería…?"), or a news statement.
2. MECHANISM CHAIN: hits chain 5–6 distinct 3D setups (~8–9 s each), each
   visualising ONE STEP of a physical process (mould → trapped air →
   hairs; intermittent supply → storage → height → pressure). Flops: one
   morphing object (tornado, ~16 s/phase), UI props for an abstract rule
   (law), or a static results table (soccer, ~18–20 s/shot).
3. NUMBERS ARE VISUALISED BY THE PROCESS in hits (170 °C, 10× pressure,
   1 mm vent holes; half of homes, 1 in 7) — in flops numbers are merely
   spoken (300 km/h, a 447–0 vote).
4. CLOSING: hits end with a REFRAME that RENAMES the object ("they are not
   hairs, they are the print of each air bubble"; "a water tower in
   miniature") + follow + send to a SPECIFIC person defined by a concrete
   behaviour. Tornado/law reels have a vaguer archetype; the soccer reel
   has NO send closing at all.
5. The tornado flop also shows its FINISHED phenomenon in the first frame,
   then explains — the reveal order is inverted vs the hits, which build
   the object up.
6. Localisation is a multiplier WITHIN the promise (daily-life systems of
   one large country). The owner's strategy decision (see below) adapts
   this as pan-Hispanic: objects and mechanisms that exist identically in
   every Spanish-speaking country, neutral Spanish, no country-only
   systems, no local slang.

## Cross-account synthesis — what transfers to our factory

1. INSIDE-SHOT DYNAMISM: transformation arcs, camera travel/orbit, objects
   multiplying/exploding, x-ray/cross-section reveals with labels as
   Editor overlays (never model-generated text).
2. SUBTITLES: top band (~30% of frame height), whole current phrase visible,
   current word inside a dark rounded box.
3. HOOK: "¿Por qué…?" + everyday object + a false belief to bust, in the
   first sentence; build the object up, do not show the finished phenomenon
   first.
4. STRUCTURE: one hero object OR one mechanism chain; each scene = one step
   of the process; numbers carried by animated data overlays, not just spoken.
5. CLOSING: spoken reframe that renames the object + follow + "send this to
   that specific friend who…". Dilemmas repeat the opening question.
6. AUDIO: voice-led, fast continuous narration, no prominent music bed,
   clip audio admitted only when it is genuine mechanism foley.
7. CAPTIONS/DESCRIPTIONS: written as mini-hooks (Ingenio style), not bare
   links.
8. TOPIC FAMILIES, ranked by evidence across the three accounts + Zack D
   Films' back catalogue:
   a. Everyday objects with a hidden mechanism (tape measure hook, plug
      holes, keyboard zones, touchscreen edges, tire hairs, escalator
      brush, pregnancy-test gold) — the broadest proven family, and the
      most "verifiable on the spot".
   b. Body / medical "how it works" (stitches, splinters, blood vessels —
      Zack's global champions; pregnancy test bridges body + object).
   c. Impossible numbers / scale (chess games, Rubik permutations, paper
      to the Moon, gear ratios) — the most code-3D-friendly family.
   d. What-if / dilemmas (comment engines; lower likes, extreme comment
      rates — use deliberately, not as the base).
   AVOID: generic textbook science with no everyday anchor (tornado, GPS
   flopped everywhere), news/sports/labour-law drift (off-promise).

## Channel strategy decided by the owner (Oct 25, 2026)

- Audience: pan-Hispanic Spanish speakers. NOT Chile-only (owner: "Chile is
  very small, not worth it"). Neutral Spanish, universal topics; no
  country-specific systems or local slang in scripts.
- ONE umbrella channel first: the promise is "why/how the things you see
  and use every day work", rotating the four evidence-backed families above
  under ONE format, voice and brand. The recommendation algorithm clusters
  by audience-and-promise; Shorts test each video largely on its own
  merits, so rotation inside one promise widens the viral surface.
  Off-promise drift is what actually hurt the references.
- Multiple channels are the owner's stated end-state, but spin-offs happen
  LATER and only by data: a family that consistently wins AND can sustain
  its own publishing cadence, or the English-language version. Never
  micro-topic channel splits from day one.
- The factory must therefore treat "channel" as configuration (channel.yaml:
  voice, language, style, topic-family mix), not as hard-coded assumptions.

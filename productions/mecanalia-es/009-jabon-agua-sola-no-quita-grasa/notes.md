## Sources and numbers (no number is invented; none is rendered as a source on screen)
- Soap molecules are amphiphiles: a hydrophilic head and a hydrophobic tail; they surround grease as micelles that stay suspended in water and rinse away: standard chemistry.
- Water molecules attract each other (hydrogen bonds), which is why grease is pushed together: drawn as mint bonds.

## Scientific simplifications and deviations (stated plainly)
- Heads and tails are balls and sticks, sizes not to scale; real micelles hold hundreds of molecules and are far smaller; the video shows eight droplets for "miles".
- The drop breaking when rubbed is a choreographed split, not a simulation.
- "Se lleva bien" is plain language for hydrophilic/hydrophobic.

## Lens record
- Marketing (lead on topic): curiosity 9/10: The 'siete años' warning is a childhood myth across the Spanish-speaking world; the journey is hidden and slightly gross, and the turn has real danger.
- Scriptwriter: flatness test passed (each scene's 'what changes' is a different event; swapping any two breaks the story); the turn comes before the closing reframe; the archetype names a behaviour.
- Design: code3d on the asset route: a stylised digestive-system asset (body family, cream cutaway plate) is the stage; the gum stick and wrapper are geometric; the chewed piece is read through its wrapper and the gut around it. Variant journey (follow one piece); the family default is fork, but the mechanism is a route, so journey. Preset chosen at scene design.

## Sources and numbers (no number is invented; none is rendered as a source on screen)
- Myth and passage: Scientific American 2007, 'Fact or Fiction?: Chewing Gum Takes Seven Years to Digest' (gum base is indigestible but passes with the stool in days). Verified (search result).
- Bezoars: Milov, Andres, Erhart & Bailey 1998, Pediatrics 102(2):e22, 'Chewing Gum Bezoars of the Gastrointestinal Tract': children who habitually swallowed gum (one swallowed 5-7 pieces a day) formed gum bezoars with obstruction/constipation. Verified (PubMed abstract and coverage).
- Sugar and flavour dissolve, base does not; gum base is polymer/rubber-like; corn hulls pass undigested: standard digestive physiology, FLAGGED as general knowledge, to cite before the beat sheet is final.
- 'Varios metros de intestino' and 'pocos días': deliberately vague, no number on screen except the crossed-out '7 años' (the myth itself).

## Scientific simplifications (stated plainly)
- The digestive tract is a stylised cutaway; the gum is enlarged.
- Transit time varies widely between people; the narration says 'pocos días'.

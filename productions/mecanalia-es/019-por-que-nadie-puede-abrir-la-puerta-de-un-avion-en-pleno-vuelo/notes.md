## Sources and numbers (no number is invented; none is rendered as a source on screen; FLAGGED items are confirmed or removed at the beat sheet)
- Plug-type passenger doors are larger than their opening; cabin pressure presses them into the frame, and opening needs an inward move first: TravelPulse 'Why an airplane door won't open in mid-flight'; Business Insider 2020 (same explanation); A320 door description (plug-type, opening upward, outward and forward). FLAGGED: confirm against a manufacturer or regulator text at the beat sheet.
- Cabin pressurisation exists so occupants can breathe at cruise altitude: SKYbrary 'Aircraft Pressurisation Systems'.
- Differential pressure: Boeing 737 MAX FCOM schedule, 7.45 psid at or below 28,000 ft, 7.80 psid from 28,000 to 37,000 ft, 8.35 psid above 37,000 ft (live search 2026-11-11). 7.45 psid = 51.4 kPa.
- Door area about 0.8 m x 1.8 m = 1.44 m2 (FLAGGED: confirm in Boeing 737 ACAP 'Door clearances, forward main entry door' or Airbus A320 Aircraft Characteristics 'Forward passenger/crew doors'). Arithmetic: 51.4 kPa x 1.44 m2 = 74 kN = about 7.5 t-force (8.35 psid gives about 8.5 t). Narration says 'unas siete toneladas', the low end. If the area cannot be sourced, rung 3 below removes the number.
- Five cars: 7.5 t / about 1.4-1.5 t per mid-size car = 5. FLAGGED: source one common sedan's kerb weight at the beat sheet.
- Turn: Alaska Airlines flight 1282, 5 January 2024, Boeing 737-9: the left mid-exit door plug separated about six minutes after take-off from Portland; the four bolts that secure it were missing when the aircraft left the factory (NTSB final report, press release NR20250624, 24 June 2025; AvWeb and Airline Geeks coverage, July 2025). The two seats beside the plug were unoccupied (widely reported; FLAGGED to confirm in the NTSB report before staging empty seats).

## Scientific simplifications (stated plainly)
- The door's stops are drawn larger than real for legibility.
- Air under pressure is drawn as glowing accent air; real air is invisible.
- The 'panel tapón' is a plug fitted where an optional emergency exit can go, not a passenger door; the narration calls it a panel, not a door.
